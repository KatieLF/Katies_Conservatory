**Ownership:** the act of owning something, the power to change or modify.
**Rent:** a usually fixed sum of money made by a tenant or user to the owner for the possession and use thereof

When a person buys a house, they get to do whatever they like with it. They can paint the walls, change the layout, or even modify the infrastructure to make it better fit their lifestyle. This used to be the same for technology, specifically media. You could change the format of the media to make it better fit your lifestyle (say, ripping a DVD you already own to put it onto your smart phone).

But now, even if you buy something at full price digitally, you can't own it. You are only renting it for as long as you use it within that digital system. You can't edit it or take it out of the format even if you wanted to.

Part of the reason this irks me is the language surrounding these digital stores. It is labeled "buying" when in actuality it is "renting". For the same price it would be to buy a physical copy in store, it is to rent. I don't mind the renting aspect, I mind that the language is not honest!

So what am I doing to make myself an owner?

- I start a [[JellyFin]] with all my parent's physical movies.
- I use a [[UGREEN NAS]] as my digital file storage.
- I buy physical versions of things I want to own, like Project Hail Mary.
- I buy from the source, i.e. buying from authors or venders directly instead of through a digital storefront (the one exception I have personally is Steam)
- Choose good hardware to support this ownership like a Kobo e-reader, a dumb TV that only has access to Jellyfin, and a physical game emulator like Miyoo for old ROMs.

In the grand scheme of things, does this effect the larger world all that much? No. Does this make me more conscious of my choices? Absolutely! I am more aware of my tools, where I spend my money, and where I am spending my time.
